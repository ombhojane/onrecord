Video limit: **3:00**. Aim for 2:45 so you have room for safety.
Rule for the whole video: **show it, don't claim it.** Every number on screen should be visible in the product or on the explorer.

## The one line

> **"Paste a tipster's channel. See their real record, including the calls they deleted."**

## The 15-second version (for the Devfolio tagline or a hallway pitch)

> "93% of Indian F&O traders lose money, and many of them follow Telegram tip channels whose track records are screenshots the channel can edit or delete. OnRecord writes every public message to Arkiv the moment it appears, so deletions show up as tombstones and profit claims get checked against the calls that were actually posted beforehand. Nobody can edit the record. Not the tipster, not a platform, not us."

## The core insight (memorize this line, it wins the Q&A)

> **"Every fraud in this market is a database edit."**
> A call's outcome is public, because anyone can check a price chart. What the tipster controls is *which calls count*. They delete the losers, run five channels and keep the one that got lucky, and post "PROFIT BOOKED ✅" for trades that were never announced. If you take away their ability to edit the record, the trick stops working.

## Product flow (how to explain it in a live Q&A)

**1. The trader about to pay (the main user)**
Sees a channel promoting "₹2,200/month premium" → pastes the handle into OnRecord → gets the integrity scoreboard in seconds: committed calls, unbacked profit claims, deletions → decides with evidence instead of screenshots.
*Free, with no login or wallet, in 60 seconds.*

**2. The subscriber who got burned (spreads the product)**
Lost money following a channel → adds it to the watchlist ("Start witnessing") → shares the scoreboard link in Reddit, X, and Telegram threads where victims already warn each other.
*They already post these warnings. We give the warnings proof.*

**3. The honest analyst (who will eventually pay)**
Is buried among fraudsters and has no way to prove the difference → posts calls where OnRecord can witness them → shares the clean record: "every call committed beforehand, zero deletions."
*This is the Carfax model, where the honest seller pays to prove it. It covers the call-level gap that PaRRVA, which works at portfolio level for registered advisors only, doesn't.*

**4. Journalists and regulators**
SEBI orders take years to reconstruct a channel's history. OnRecord's graded, timestamped history is an evidence pack ready on day one.

## Judge Q&A: the tough questions and your answers

**"Why not just screenshot or archive the channel?"**
"A screenshot proves nothing, because it's yours to edit. Wayback can be asked to remove content and isn't structured or queryable. We need a record that is permissionless, queryable by channel and type, and owned by nobody. That's the whole product."

**"Can't the tipster just make the channel private?"**
"Yes. Then they've stopped selling publicly, and we show that the preview is restricted. Hiding your record becomes part of your record. Either way, the trader learns something."

**"What about image-only P&L screenshots?"**
"We witness them as hashes today. OCR is next. A screenshot posted after the fact still counts as a claim with no committed call behind it."

**"Isn't this defamation risk?"**
"We publish no opinions, only what a channel broadcast publicly and when it disappeared. Every row links to the chain. We describe what we observed, and we don't call anyone a fraud."

**"How is this different from Zerodha Verified P&L or SEBI's PaRRVA?"**
"Zerodha verifies one account at one broker, over a window the user picks, and it's opt-in. PaRRVA verifies portfolios of about two thousand *registered* advisors. Neither verifies the *call stream* of an unregistered Telegram channel, which is where the losses happen. We're the complement, and we don't need anyone to enroll."

**"Your classifier is heuristic. What about false positives?"**
"Call detection is deliberately strict. It needs an entry plus a target or stop-loss. When in doubt we under-count calls, and that can only make a channel look *more* honest, never less. Precision matters more than recall when you're judging someone's record."
